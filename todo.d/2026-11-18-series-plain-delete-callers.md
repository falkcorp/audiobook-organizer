- [ ] **SERIES-PLAIN-DELETE-CALLERS** Five direct non-test call sites still delete a series with the plain `DeleteSeries`, which takes only the series lock and no book write lock, so it can strand a book that a writer links between the caller's count and the delete (the new existence check in `createBook` and `updateBookLockedMode` narrows that window but cannot close it for an unconditional delete): `internal/dedup/series_dedup.go` (lines 698 and 1150), `internal/server/duplicates_helpers.go` (651), `internal/server/handlers/entities/handler.go` (1174, 1209). `internal/dedup/series_prune_core.go` also calls it through its store argument; the Repairs fixer now passes a store whose `DeleteSeries` is the check-and-delete, but the whole-library prune op in `internal/server/duplicates_helpers.go` (`executeSeriesPrune`) passes the real store. Move each onto `DeleteSeriesIfUnreferenced`, then drop `DeleteSeries` from the contracts that no longer need it.
- [ ] **SERIES-DENUMBER-REVERT-SCAN-STAND-DOWN** The revert of a `maintenance.series-denumber` run takes the registry's write-set hold (PR 4716), so it no longer runs beside the other registry operations that write the same resources. It does not stand the library scan down, and that leaves a concrete race: the revert restores each book's pre-run `series_id` (and recreates the series it deleted, `revertSeriesCreate`) as a per-book compare-and-set, while a scan of the same folder reads a book, resolves its series through get-or-create and writes the row back under the same book stripe. A scan that read a book before the revert and writes after it puts the denumbered series link back on a book the revert just restored (or the reverse: the revert's compare-and-set loses and reports the book as changed since the run). Neither write is lost silently, but a revert that must restore a whole run can leave books on the wrong series with only a per-book 'changed since' line to show for it. HTTP requests were deliberately taken out of the scan stand-down on 2026-09-30 and coordinate with the scanner per book through `internal/scanlock`; decide whether a revert that restores a whole run's series links should take the stand-down lease the way a dispatched maintenance op does, or whether per-book coordination is enough, and pin the answer with a test that runs a scan against a revert.
- [ ] **SERIES-WRITER-GUARD-RESIDUE** Two review leftovers from PR 4716 that are known and accepted, not fixed. (1) The revert write-set hold answers 409 for as long as any registry operation declaring `ResBooks` runs, and 18 definitions do (among them `acoustid.backfill`, `maintenance.transcribe-book-intros`, `maintenance.repair-library-state` and `maintenance.rewrite-path-prefix`), some for hours. There is no queue and no estimate: the 409 names the blocking operation, and the operator has to retry. Decide whether the UI should say it can be hours, or whether the revert should wait (a queued hold) instead of refusing. Operations registered with no `Writes` (`maintenance.cleanup-series`, see MAINTENANCE-JOB-OPS-REGISTER-WITHOUT-WRITES) are still invisible to the hold in both directions. (2) The split and copy sites that drop a deleted series (`DropDanglingSeriesRef` in `versions.go`, `transcode_version.go`, `split_book_fill_empty.go`, `service_apply.go`, `reconcile.go`) look outside the book's stripe, before `CreateBook` or inside the `ModifyBook` callback's own check, so a series delete that lands between the look and the write still makes the write refuse: SplitVersion unwinds its split group and answers 500, split-file reports a failure, the M4B record falls back to updating the original. The window is narrow and fails closed. Consider retrying once on `ErrSeriesNotFound` after dropping again.
