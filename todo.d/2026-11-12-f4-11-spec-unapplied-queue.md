- [ ] **F4-11-SPEC-UNAPPLIED-QUEUE** Amend 05-backup section 5B.3.6 row "Boot re-apply" and section 5B.9 with what F4-11's follow-up built for a revocation line of a kind the build does not declare (the journal's forward compatibility, replacing the stop-and-refuse rule). (1) Such a line is recorded in a durable queue (`c/backup/journal_unapplied/<seq>`, an entry is a pointer: seq, mac, kind, at, closed) BEFORE the first batch that can move the head, the head then passes it, and the journal is neither refused nor reported broken. (2) The first boot of a build that declares the kind applies the line, only when the verified chain holds that seq with that mac and the chain is whole, and deletes the entry in the batch that applies it; an entry the chain does not hold, or any entry on a chain that is not whole, waits and is counted and logged at every boot. (3) A plain boot fails closed once per entry (the store may lack a revocation it cannot name) and marks the entries answered; a restore boot leaves the pass to its hooks and the next plain boot. (4) The restriction of a kept last admin is journaled only after the whole tail is applied and queued, and the line that kept the admin completes (its head, its queue entry's removal) only after the restriction, so no append moves the head past a line not yet handled. (5) No format version in the journal header is needed for line kinds: an unknown kind costs one fail-closed pass and a knowing build applies it; a format version remains a separate safeguard for a change of the file's layout. (6) audit's copy-forward key is the restore line's seq, or a per-run ID (`c/backup/restore_run`) for a restore whose line could not be journaled, never seq 0. Optional refinement left out: a declared-skippable kind rule so a pure bookkeeping kind costs no pass.
