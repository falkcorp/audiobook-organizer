### Security

#### A direct LAN peer now counts against the bootstrap and invite global rate ceilings

The bootstrap limiter and the public invite limiter exempted a "direct LAN peer" from their global ceiling, so internet traffic spending the ceiling could not lock out an owner on the LAN. Direct only means the TCP peer is on a private address and sent no Cloudflare header; with no host firewall in front of the port, any LAN host or container bridge qualifies, so the exemption handed every such caller request budget that tunnel visitors could not touch. The exemption mechanism is gone (`bootstrapLimiter.allow` no longer takes an exemption flag, `bootstrapClientKey` no longer returns one, and the invite middleware always takes a global slot). A direct peer keeps its own per-address bucket and its LAN aggregates, which now apply on top of the ceiling, and loopback counts too. The cost is that a flood spending the ceiling can refuse the owner's recovery until the window ends; the bootstrap limiter is in memory, so restarting the service (the first step of the bootstrap flow) clears it.
