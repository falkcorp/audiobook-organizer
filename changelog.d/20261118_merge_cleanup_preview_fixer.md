### Added

- A read-only Repairs fixer, `maintenance.merge-cleanup` ("Fix mistaken merges", Duplicates group, last in the group), lists every book that carries a merge pointer but is not in the trash and still has a live file, and says what each merge really is. It is `PlanOnly` and has no apply path: nothing is written to books, files, journals or decisions, and a library scan is not stood down for it. Each row is one of three kinds. `unmerge`: the two books are different works, meaning at least two of three wrong-work signals (the titles share nothing, the credit lists share no name, or the books sit at different positions in the same series) and none of the four reasons to doubt them (the titles match, an alternative title of one matches a title of the other, the single files share a hash or an original hash, or the runtimes match to the second and the sizes to a thousandth). `swap_keeper`: the same work, but the target runs under 80% of the loser's length, so the target is a fragment and the loser is the copy to keep (a target about half the loser's length, or under thirty minutes, is held as `runtime_ambiguous` instead). `repoint_chain`: the pointer leads into the trash or to a book that does not exist, and exactly one live book is reachable another way: the dead book's version group, or the holder of its retired file rows, and a holder or group member that was itself merged away is followed to the live book it was merged into. A repoint is checked like any other merge: only a target the signals call the same work passes, and the owner's rejects are read against it. A merge that looks correct, or cannot be told, is listed as `no_verdict` and left alone.
- The verdict reads a book's title, alternative titles, credits (compared as sets of folded names, so a composite credit is read as its names), series position, ASIN, file count, runtime and file hashes. When a fact is missing or ambiguous the row is held and never classed as an unmerge or a swap: a placeholder title or credit (`placeholder_metadata`), a credit naming an author that does not exist (`dangling_author`), a book whose credit is the other book's title, whichever of the two is stored swapped (`title_author_swapped`, repaired by "Fix swapped title and author"), a file with no measured length (`runtime_incomplete`) and a file with no recorded size where the identical-runtime check needs it (`size_incomplete`). The verdict is made on the audio that is on disk; when the chapter rows that are off disk would change it, the row is held as `runtime_includes_missing` and both runtimes are shown. A dead end looks for a live book from every book whose content may have gone on from the dead one: the pointer-carrying hops of the loser's own chain (a live merged book with no file of its own as much as one in the trash), and every other book whose chain enters the same dead book, live or in the trash, with or without a file, together with the pointer-carrying hops on their chains. For each such source it reads the holders of its retired versions, the primary of its version group, and the members of that group that carry a merge pointer (the soft-deleted members that still point on are read from the trash, since the group read leaves them out), and follows each of those along its own merge chain to the live book it leads to; what an anchor's own holders or group hold is not expanded. The loser's own version group is not read through another book (the group gate covers it). A holder or group member that is not in the trash, has a file, and whose own chain ends at a dead book is a live copy of the work, not nothing. The group is accounted for by its primary alone: a live, unmerged member beside a primary that led to a live book is that primary's sibling, while one beside no usable primary is a second place the loser may belong. The loser is proposed to stand alone only when none of those leads to a live book, no live copy is left behind the dead end, and the dead book's version group has no live, unmerged book left; a group with one (no primary elected, two unflagged, or the loser itself the primary) holds the row as `repoint_group_no_primary`, a loop or a chain over the cap holds it as `repoint_ambiguous`, and two or more live books merged into the same dead end, or a live copy that leads to a dead end too (listed under `dead_end_live_copies` in the row's current values), hold the row as `dead_end_shared` instead of each standing alone. A dead-end unmerge proposes the loser's version group and primary flag as `unchanged`, and the group gate treats it as staying in its group, so a loser that would be its group's only live member with no primary is held as `group_needs_handoff`; a loser whose own flag reads as primary (nil counts) is its group's primary and is not held; a cycle head is treated the same way. The dead-end index is built on a bounded worker pool. A repoint row shows its target the way an unmerge or swap row shows its own (title, credits, runtime, files, the signals read) and the target's files are part of the row's fingerprint. A chain is followed by its own pointer walker, not `ResolveMergeSurvivor`, which returns the first non-trashed book and so would compare a loser with another loser; the verdict is made against the live end of the chain, and a chain through another merged book is only flagged (`chain_via_merged`), not repointed. A cycle unmerges one member (longest known runtime, then most files, then oldest, then lowest id) and holds the rest until a re-plan.
- Holds are listed on every row, in the order the gates ran, and the first is the row's skip kind: `handled_by_version_group_repair` (the version-group fixer's own `planGroup` would write a book this row would write, so the two fixers never plan the same book), `retired_shell`, `cross_group`, `swap_target_not_primary`, `swap_would_not_crown_keeper` (the swap is simulated through `reviveMembers` and `versionprimary.Elect`), `group_needs_handoff`, `sync_redirect_present`, `sync_redirect_broken` (a redirect chain that cannot be followed), `owner_says_distinct` and `owner_veto_unresolvable` (these hold a swap or a repoint, which say the two books are one work; on an unmerge the owner's reject agrees with the proposal and is only a flag), plus the verdict skips and the chain skips. The facts later work needs are also row flags, which count across every class even when an earlier hold settled the row: `group_needs_handoff`, `sync_redirect_present`, `sync_redirect_broken`, `owner_says_distinct`, `chain_via_merged`, `chain_via_trashed`.
- Every row carries origin evidence, retired shells and rows whose read failed included: `origin_match4` (the pointer, an explicit false primary flag and a metadata-source hash shared with the target, which is the only thing MATCH-4 writes), `origin_match4_partial` (the pointer and flag but no shared hash), `origin_other`, or `origin_unknown` when the book or the book it points at could not be read, so the four add up to the rows. It is evidence only; a pointer is repaired the same whoever wrote it. Each row also shows both books' credits, series position, ASIN and alternative titles. The plan lists books through the authoritative listing and refuses, with a logged warning that gives the counts, when the in-memory book index holds fewer books than that listing. It logs one census line with the merged-book count, rows, exclusions (trashed, no live file, missing, no longer merged) and the counts by class, by first skip kind, by every hold (a row held twice counts under both) and by flag.
- The plan classifies the merged books on a bounded worker pool (`min(NumCPU, 8)`, `registry.RunItems` with `Concurrency` set) over per-run caches of books, groups, file hashes and credits. Its progress label reads an atomic counter, because `RunItems` calls Label inside the workers.
- `RepairsPanel` words every new skip kind, class and flag; the deep-link reference lists them.
