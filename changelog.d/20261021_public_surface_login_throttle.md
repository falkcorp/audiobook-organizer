### Security

#### Login failure throttle now counts each visitor behind the tunnel separately

The web login and the Audiobookshelf-compatible `/login` and `/auth/refresh` throttled failed attempts per peer address. Behind the Cloudflare tunnel the peer is always the connector, so one visitor failing 15 times locked every other visitor out, and a visitor could not be told apart from another. The throttle now keys on the visitor address (`CF-Connecting-IP`, believed only from a configured tunnel connector or a verified Access assertion; a public IPv6 visitor counts per /64). A request from any other peer is still keyed on its own address, so a spoofed header cannot pick a bucket. Session and audit records are unchanged.

The per-visitor budget does nothing against a caller with many addresses, so password login now has aggregate ceilings in front of it: 150 failures per 15 minutes through any one configured tunnel connector, and 500 across the whole service, shared by the web `POST /api/v1/auth/login` and the Audiobookshelf-compatible `POST /login` (IPv6 visitors still count per /64). Only failed (401) attempts and attempts still in flight count. A successful login takes back only the failures that same visitor made against the same account, from the last 15 minutes and at most five of them; failures against any other account stay counted at the visitor, connector and service levels, so one valid account cannot be used to clear the count and keep guessing at others. A spent ceiling does not refuse logins, because that would let an anonymous flood lock out every user, the owner included. A request carrying a verified Cloudflare Access identity (a JWT verified against the team keys and AUD, for a verified, allow-listed email) is exempt from the ceilings and keeps its per-visitor and per-account limits. Any other login is delayed before password work, by one second plus half a second per failure already on its account, capped at three seconds, with no lock held while it waits; only an account that has already failed five times in the window, or a visitor past its own budget, gets a 429. Delayed requests are bounded per visitor (two at a time, one when the service holds more than 256 of them) rather than by a shared pool a flood could fill, and a visitor that has already failed while a ceiling is spent is refused outright until the window ends, so a flood from many addresses cannot stop a fresh visitor's first attempt on an untouched account; a high service-wide bound only protects goroutines. The cost that remains: while a ceiling is spent, a stranger's guesses at one account can take it past five failures and refuse that account's password login (not its Cloudflare path) until the window ends. Sessions already issued are unaffected and a restart clears the state. `POST /auth/refresh` stays outside the ceilings because it does no password work. The per-visitor and per-account failure tables are also capped at 10,000 keys each with the oldest window evicted first, and expired entries are swept once a minute instead of on every failure.
