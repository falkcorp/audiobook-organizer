### Security

#### Login failure throttle now counts each visitor behind the tunnel separately

The web login and the Audiobookshelf-compatible `/login` and `/auth/refresh` throttled failed attempts per peer address. Behind the Cloudflare tunnel the peer is always the connector, so one visitor failing 15 times locked every other visitor out, and a visitor could not be told apart from another. The throttle now keys on the visitor address (`CF-Connecting-IP`, believed only from a configured tunnel connector or a verified Access assertion; a public IPv6 visitor counts per /64). A request from any other peer is still keyed on its own address, so a spoofed header cannot pick a bucket. Session and audit records are unchanged.

The per-visitor budget does nothing against a caller with many addresses, so password login now has aggregate ceilings in front of it: 150 failures per 15 minutes through any one configured tunnel connector, and 500 across the whole service, shared by the web `POST /api/v1/auth/login` and the Audiobookshelf-compatible `POST /login` (IPv6 visitors still count per /64). Only failed (401) attempts and attempts still in flight count.

Both logins now share ONE failed-login counter per username. It is keyed on the normalised username the caller SUBMITTED (trimmed, lower-cased, at most 128 bytes), whether or not that user exists, so a guess through either door counts toward the same account and an unknown name is counted and slowed exactly like a real one. A successful login takes back only what it earned: the failures that same visitor made against that same account in the last 15 minutes, at most five of them, at the visitor, connector and service levels and in the per-source budget. It no longer clears the visitor's whole counter, so a caller holding one valid account cannot log in between rounds of guesses at others to reset its budget; failures against any other account stay counted. Mistyping your own password and then getting in still costs nothing.

A spent ceiling does not refuse logins, because that would let an anonymous flood lock out every user, the owner included. A request carrying a verified Cloudflare Access identity (a JWT verified against the team keys and AUD, for a verified, allow-listed email) is exempt from the ceilings and keeps its per-visitor and per-account limits. Any other login is delayed before password work, by one second plus half a second per failure already on its account, capped at three seconds, with no lock held while it waits. Whether a saturated login is refused with a 429 is decided by the submitted username before the account is looked up, so a name that exists and one that does not get the same answer: a username that has already failed five times in the window, or a visitor past its own budget, is refused. A refusal counts as a failure while saturated, like a 401: that visitor gets no further attempt until the window ends. Delayed requests are bounded per visitor (two at a time, one when the service holds more than 256 of them) rather than by a shared pool a flood could fill, so a flood from many addresses cannot stop a fresh visitor's first attempt on an untouched account; a high service-wide bound only protects goroutines.

The login body is now read before anything is counted, under a 4 KiB cap and a 5 second read deadline, and handed to the handler from memory: a caller that opens a login and stalls its body is answered 408 and holds neither a ceiling slot nor a place in the handlers, and an oversized body is answered 413. One visitor may also have at most four logins in the handlers at once (a fifth is answered 429 with `Retry-After`), on both logins. A login body over 4 KiB, such as an Audiobookshelf `deviceInfo` of unusual size, is therefore refused.

The cost that remains: while a ceiling is spent, a stranger's guesses at one account can take it past five failures and refuse that account's password login (not its Cloudflare path) until the window ends. Sessions already issued are unaffected and a restart clears the state. `POST /auth/refresh` stays outside the ceilings because it does no password work, and HTTP Basic auth on the API is a separate path that is not throttled here. The per-visitor and per-username tables are bounded at 10,000 entries each, kept in least-recently-written order: entries idle for a whole window are swept on every write, and at the bound the oldest entry is dropped in constant time instead of scanning the table.
